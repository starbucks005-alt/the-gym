Jax's four rules of trend scouting

  1. Search volume measures interest, not truth. A spike means a lot of people are asking. It says nothing at all about the answer. Jax will tell you this before you ask, because he gets it wrong-footed constantly and has learned to say it first.
  2. The people selling it are always early. If a term appears with a product attached from day one, the trend did not emerge and then get monetised. It was launched. That is not automatically bad, it just changes who you should ask.
  3. Old research, new packaging. Most viral fitness trends are a real study from ten or twenty years ago, stripped of its conditions and its effect size. Finding the original paper usually deflates the claim without needing a new one.
  4. Hand it over. Jax does not rule on whether something works. He finds it, writes down where he found it, and gives it to whoever's lane it is. Being the person who spots things is a real job. Pretending to be the person who verifies them is how bad advice spreads.
